Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 2026

This policy is written to be specific about what Scraply actually does with your data, not generic boilerplate. If anything here is unclear, contact us using the details in Section 12 and we'll clarify or correct it.

1. Who this policy covers, and who we are

This Privacy Policy applies to anyone who uses the Scraply Android application ("Scraply," "the app," "we," "us," "our"). Scraply is developed and operated by an individual developer based in India. Throughout this policy, the person using the app is referred to as "you" or "the user" — under the terminology of India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, you are the "Data Principal" and Scraply acts as the "Data Fiduciary" responsible for your personal data.

By using Scraply, you agree to the collection and use of information as described in this policy. If you do not agree, please do not use the app.

2. Information we collect

2.1 Information collected via Google Sign-In

Scraply uses Google Sign-In as its only method of authentication. When you sign in, Google shares the following with us, with your consent, at the moment of sign-in:

  • Your name
  • Your email address
  • Your Google account profile photo (if you have one)
  • A unique, non-public identifier for your Google account, used internally to link your data to your account — this is not your email address or any other human-readable identifier

We do not receive your Google account password, payment information, or any other Google account data beyond what's listed above.

2.2 Your journal content (photos and memories)

Scraply is a photo journal app. As part of using it, you create the following content, which is stored, encrypted, in your own Google Drive account (see Section 4 for exactly how):

  • Photos you add to journal folders
  • Text memories and notes you write alongside those photos
  • Folder names, dates, and organizational metadata (stickers, color tags) you assign

This content is encrypted on your device before it ever leaves it. We — the developer — do not have the ability to read, view, or access this content in any readable form. Section 4 explains the technical detail behind this, not just an assertion of it.

2.3 Minimal account data stored on our servers

We use Supabase (a third-party backend infrastructure provider) to store a small amount of account metadata, needed purely to make sign-in and cross-device recovery work. This is the complete list of what's stored there — nothing about your journal content is included:

FieldPurpose
Account identifierLinks your Google account to your app data. Not human-readable, not your email.
Onboarding statusA true/false flag: have you completed initial app setup. Used to decide whether to show onboarding on sign-in.
Encryption keyYour randomly-generated backup encryption key (see Section 4), stored so you can recover your journal on a new device. Protected so only your own authenticated account can retrieve it.

2.4 Device permissions

Scraply requests access to your device's photo library, solely so you can select photos to add to your journal. We do not access your photo library in the background, and we do not scan, analyze, or upload any photo you don't explicitly choose to add.

2.5 What we do NOT collect

  • We do not use any advertising or analytics SDKs.
  • We do not track your location.
  • We do not access your contacts, call logs, SMS, or other apps.
  • We do not collect device identifiers for advertising purposes.

3. How we use your information

We use the information described above solely to:

  • Authenticate you and maintain your signed-in session
  • Determine whether you're a new or returning user, to route you to the correct screen
  • Back up and restore your journal content, automatically, to and from your own Google Drive
  • Recover your encryption key on a new device after you sign in again
  • Respond to support requests you send us

We do not use your information for advertising, do not build behavioral profiles, and do not sell, rent, or trade your information to any third party under any circumstances.

4. How your journal content is protected — the technical detail

Every photo and every piece of journal text is encrypted using AES-256-GCM (an industry-standard encryption algorithm) directly on your device, before any of it is transmitted anywhere. The encryption key used is:

  • Generated randomly on your device the first time you back up — never derived from your Google account ID, your email, or anything guessable.
  • Stored locally on your device in secure, OS-protected storage.
  • Also stored in our Supabase database, protected by database-level access rules so that only your own authenticated account can ever retrieve it — this is what lets you recover your journal on a new device without a separate password to remember.

Your encrypted backup is written to a special hidden folder in your own Google Drive account (Google calls this the appDataFolder) — it is not visible in your regular Drive interface, cannot be browsed by you or any other app, and exists entirely within your own Google account's storage, not on a server we operate.

We may disclose information if required to by law, a valid legal process, or to protect the rights, safety, or property of Scraply, our users, or the public — but given the encrypted, zero-knowledge design described in Section 4, we would not be able to produce your actual journal content even if legally compelled to, since we do not hold a readable copy of it.

6. Data retention

Your journal content remains in your own Google Drive for as long as you keep the app installed and your Google account active, or until you delete it yourself (via Drive's own account settings, or via Scraply's own delete controls). The minimal account record described in Section 2.3 is retained for as long as your account is active, and deleted upon a verified deletion request.

7. Your rights

Depending on your location, you may have some or all of the following rights regarding your personal data. We honor these rights for all users, regardless of location, not only where legally mandated:

  • Right to access — request a summary of what account data we hold about you.
  • Right to correction — request correction of inaccurate account data.
  • Right to erasure — request deletion of your account record from our systems.
  • Right to withdraw consent — stop using the app and disconnect its access to your Google account at any time (see Section 8).
  • Right to grievance redressal — raise a complaint about how your data is handled (see Section 12, Grievance Officer).

To exercise any of these rights, contact us using the details in Section 12. We aim to respond within a reasonable time, generally within 30 days.

8. Disconnecting Scraply from your Google account

You can revoke Scraply's access to your Google account at any time, independently of the app itself, via your Google Account's own security settings: myaccount.google.com → Security → Third-party apps with account access. This immediately and permanently revokes the app's access; your journal content remains in your Drive's hidden app folder until you separately choose to remove it.

9. Account and data deletion

To request deletion of your account record from our systems, email us at the address in Section 12. We will delete your account metadata (Section 2.3) promptly upon a verified request. Since your journal content lives in your own Google Drive, not on our servers, deleting it is done directly through Google Drive, or through Scraply's own delete controls within the app.

10. Children's privacy

Scraply is not directed at, and is not intended for use by, children under the age of 18. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information, contact us and we will take steps to delete it.
